Precedents for Re-execution
The repro manifest, repro.yaml, lists each command to rerun, the input files the
command reads, and the numbers the manuscript prints from its output. repro reproduce
reruns each listed command in a directory that holds only that command's declared inputs, and
then compares every number the manuscript prints from the output with the value at its declared
address. Table sets each
of its design choices beside the closest earlier system and states the difference. Most choices
have a direct precedent. Three do not, in the sources read: an outcome for a rerun whose number
could not be read, a label on a step that ran after an upstream failure, and a comparison of a
printed value with a declared address in an output file that stays with the project.
Design choices of repro reproduce, the closest precedent for each, and the
difference. Rerunning is common and is specified in many ways; a mechanical comparison against
the numbers a manuscript prints, with outcomes that keep a rerun that did not happen apart from
a rerun that disagreed, is where the command departs from the systems before it.

Chain-of-Evidence. ScientistOne is an autonomous
research system whose writer tags each sentence that holds a number with a line of its
experiment log, and whose audit reruns submitted code and compares the result with the reported
score. repro reproduce differs from it in five ways. (1) Who can use it. The
tags exist only for papers that ScientistOne itself writes, and its audit needs a benchmark's
own evaluator; the repro manifest is written for any project, by a person or an agent, and
lists the author's own commands. (2) What the reader receives. ScientistOne removes the tags from
the final manuscript; the manifest, the pinned inputs and the log of reruns stay in the
repository, so a reader repeats the check from a clone. (3) How a check is decided. Its
audit has a language model read the reported score out of the paper, and two of its four checks
are majority votes of a language model; every claim in the manifest is machine-checkable, in
that the number the manuscript prints is compared, as a decimal at the precision printed, with
the value at its declared address in the rerun output, and no language model takes part. (4) What is fixed beforehand. A regeneration
runs only on inputs whose bytes match their pins, and a changed input is reported as
not re-run; the ScientistOne paper describes no digest of an input or an output. (5) What a result can be.
Its numeric check passes or fails, and its authors sort the misread values by hand;
unchecked, failed and not re-run are outcomes of their own, so a check that
could not be made is never counted with those that passed. ScientistTwo applies the same audit
to its own output and reports every score verified on 49 target problems
. Its coding agent audits the repository against the manuscript, the
rerun in the one audit report the paper reproduces covers the proposed method and not the
baseline, and the paper states no tolerance for a score to count as verified.
Claims bound to files. SciTeX Clew links a manuscript claim to the session that
produced its source file and traces backward from the claim through outputs, scripts and inputs
to the raw data, verifying every hash. PaperDelta traces a manuscript's
statements to declared experiment evidence in exact decimal arithmetic and never runs the
experiment scripts. Both check a claim without a rerun. repro
reproduce reruns the declared command, and the comparison of a printed value with its declared
address is the last of three stages, so a rerun that did not happen and a value that could not be
read are outcomes of their own.
Claims as records. Blueprints represent a project's claims, evidence, assumptions and
definitions as typed graph components, and every structural rule over the graph is a warning
. propose a trace profile for autonomous
scientific agents in which a claim is an ordinary record whose evidence bindings hold which field
was read, which value was extracted and what comparison was made. repro verify makes that
comparison against the file on disk, and a failed comparison stops the build under the strict
policy.
Reproduction by an agent. SEER-Repro has a coding agent re-derive 2,190 atomic claims
of 20 published cancer-registry papers from the registry data, and reproduces 77.4% of them
. A language model extracts each reproduced value from the agent's
report, and deterministic code then applies the tolerance. The two designs start from opposite
ends. In SEER-Repro a model extracts the claims from a published paper, the reference is the
published number, and the analysis is rewritten by the agent; in the repro manifest the
author declares each claim and its address, the reference is the project's own output, and the
command that is rerun is the author's.
Audit by an agent. ReAgent audits research documents written by autonomous agents
against their repositories. Language-model agents write a claim contract
for each claim, which records the claim type, the datasets, the metrics, the settings and the
reported results, then inspect the code and rerun the experiments. On 96 agent-written
repositories its audit decisions agree with manual annotation for 70.83%, and 28 of the 96 hold
at least one static inconsistency. ReAgent asks whether the code implements the method the
document describes, which repro does not check, and repro decides the comparison
of a printed value with a file without a language model.
Formal verification in mathematics. Lean has a relatively small trusted kernel
. A proof left unfinished holds the placeholder sorry, and
#print axioms lists the axioms a theorem uses. A Lean blueprint
relates the statements of a paper to their formal counterparts: a statement is marked when it is
fully formalized, and the statements it uses form a dependency graph
. As in Lean, the check in repro is a small deterministic
program whose outcome the author does not set, and what the record does not establish is listed
beside what it does. An empirical claim has no proof, so the check is a
comparison of a printed value with a file, and repro why lists the files no command is
recorded for and the choices nothing checks.
Checklists and badges. The machine learning reproducibility checklist asks an author
to state what a paper includes, and an ACM badge records that
the main results were obtained in a later study by a team other than the authors
. Neither specifies what is rerun or
compares a printed number with an output. The repro manifest states what is rerun, as a
list of commands and the input files each reads, and the log of repro reproduce records
for every printed number whether the rerun output still holds it.
Verification in other fields. The philosophy of measurement separates an instrument's reading from the result inferred from the
reading, and an extractor is an instrument in this sense: in
Section, six extractors read the same bytes and returned six results. In formal
verification, many early automated theorem provers performed proofs without recording them, so a
result could not be checked afterwards; here every verdict is
stored with its pinned artifacts.
